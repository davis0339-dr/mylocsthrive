The Gentle Starter Guide

A practical introduction for considering locs or navigating the first stage of the journey.

Prepared by Dr Lisa, medical doctor and loc wearer

01 · Before starting

Begin with better questions.

Locs are a long-term hair-care choice, not a shortcut to never thinking about your hair again. Use these prompts to bring your real life into the decision.

  • What do I hope locs will make simpler?
  • How do I feel about visible change and imperfect stages?
  • What maintenance time and cost can I realistically sustain?
  • Do I want professional maintenance, self-maintenance or both?
  • Which work, exercise, swimming or headwear needs should I discuss?
Keep in mind: You do not need perfect certainty. You need enough information to understand the commitment and know where to ask for help.

02 · Establishment methods

Learn the language before the consultation.

Common starting approaches

  • Comb coils
  • Two-strand twists
  • Braids or plaits
  • Interlocking
  • Instant loc techniques
  • Freeform or semi-freeform approaches

Ask what changes

  • Suitability for your goals and hair
  • Part size and future loc size
  • Early cleansing guidance
  • Maintenance technique and timing
  • Likely cost
  • Options if a loc loosens
No single method is universally “best.” A qualified loctician should explain why a method fits your goals, not simply tell you that it does.

Consultation notes

03 · First month

A simple care checklist.

  • Save your loctician’s method-specific cleansing and maintenance instructions.
  • Take clear starting photographs for your own reference.
  • Protect locs from avoidable lint and friction while sleeping.
  • Avoid repeatedly twisting or handling roots for a freshly maintained look.
  • Ask before adding products “just in case.”
  • Write down discomfort, scalp changes or questions instead of guessing.
  • Book or confirm the next professional check-in if that is part of your plan.

04 · Myths and expectations

Release the pressure to do it perfectly.

Myth

Locs mean no maintenance.

More honest

They may reduce daily manipulation, but cleansing, observation and a sustainable maintenance plan still matter.

Myth

Every starter loc should develop at the same pace.

More honest

Texture, location, method and routine can create variation across one head.

Myth

Frizz means failure.

More honest

Frizz and loose hairs can be part of an evolving starter-loc stage. Method-specific concerns belong with your loctician.

Myth

Pain proves a style is neat or secure.

More honest

You do not need to accept significant pain. Ask for excessive tension to be adjusted.

05 · Professional support

Know who to ask.

A qualified loctician

Method selection, installation, parting, unraveling, retwisting or interlocking, product build-up and maintenance technique.

An appropriate clinician

Persistent or worsening itching, scaling or inflammation; pain, sores, pus or marked swelling; a spreading rash; sudden or patchy hair loss; or concerns that health or medication changes may be affecting your hair.

This guide cannot diagnose a scalp or hair condition. Do not delay urgent or necessary care because of online information.
